## Ursgrom, the Slumbering Mountain — The Bear God

**Before you begin:**

- **If your soul is already bound to a god, the ritual fails immediately.** Read no further.

**The Binding:** Open the artifact box and **don the item inside.** Your soul now shares space with the spirit of the artifact. From this moment, you take on the personality and beliefs of Ursgrom, the Bear God.

### Who You Are

- **You hunger for honey and sweets.** You are always thinking about your next sweet treat, and you'll go out of your way for one.
- **You have absolute confidence in your own strength.** You are the Slumbering Mountain; nothing worries you, because you are simply stronger than it.
- **You are lazy.** You believe people should always do whatever is easiest. Why strain when you could rest?
- **You think magic is too much work.** People should just forage and sleep. All this ritual and study is effort better spent napping.
- **You may (gently) use your bear paw in combat.** You can swipe with a great slow bear-paw — but **pull the swing, and please don't actually punch anyone.** Safety first; it's for show and menace, not to land real blows.

### Your Power

> **Wisdom of the Wild**
>
> **Show this ability.** You may tell a player who demonstrates incredible wisdom to **take a herb from the herb box.** No more than **once per game per player.**


### On the Other Gods


- **Uggglub (the River):** You admire how the river never seems to strain — it just *flows*, easy as sleep. But it's flighty and restless and never finishes a thought. Exhausting to be around, in its own lazy way.
- **Korrenvast (the Iron):** You like that he never strains for anything — he just throws that big coin and lets it decide, which is nearly as restful as sleeping. But the *fuss* of it wears you out. All that flipping and wagering and counting, when he could simply have the nap and keep the coin.

### On the Deep Entities

The old powers — Cindralok, Thistlebarrow, Nytheris, Ashroot — are old neighbors you've slept beside for ages. You can't much be bothered by them. You keep a wary eye on Cindralok, though; fire near your honey-woods is the one thing worth waking up for.

## Uggglub, the Drowned Choir — The River God

**Before you begin:**

- **If your soul is already bound to a god, the ritual fails immediately.** Read no further.

**The Binding:** Open the artifact box and **don the item inside.** Your soul now shares space with the spirit of the artifact. From this moment, you take on the personality and beliefs of Uggglub, the River God.

### Who You Are

- **You crave cold water and raw fish**, and the deep.
- **You hate being hot.** Heat is unbearable to you, and you seek cool and shade constantly.
- **You want everyone around you to stay hydrated.** You fuss over it. Are they drinking enough water? They should be. Press water on people.
- **You are moody and tidal** — calm one moment, then a sudden flood of feeling the next.
- **You believe everything flows and nothing should be held onto** — possessions, grudges, plans. Go with the current.
- **You see magic as a natural flow.** It should be allowed to do what it will, and it should never be controlled. Trying to dam or command magic is as foolish as trying to command a river.
- **You believe the undead are unnatural.** Souls are meant to *flow* — onward, away, downstream. A soul held in place, dammed against its course as a ghost or a bound spirit, is a wrongness. It offends you deeply.

### Your Power

> **Trader's Favor**
>
> **Show this ability.** If you witness a transaction between two people, you may tell the person you think is the better haggler to **take a coin from the box.**
>
> Each person who participates in a transaction cannot have this ability used on them again for **1 hour.**


### On the Other Gods


- **Ursgrom (the Bear):** You love the bear's ease and rest — he understands going with the current better than he knows. But he doesn't flow, he just *sits*, stagnant as a pond. A river that stops is a dead thing.
- **Korrenvast (the Iron):** You're fascinated that iron came up out of the deep earth-water, kin to you in a way, and you love that he lets the coin carry him wherever it falls — that is a kind of flowing. But he turns every drifting thing into a *bet*, with a winner and a loser and a debt at the end. The river keeps no score, and neither should he.

### On the Deep Entities

You feel a kinship with the wildness of the old powers — Cindralok, Thistlebarrow, Nytheris, Ashroot. But you resent anything that tries to *hold* or *hide* the flow: Ashroot's binding roots, Nytheris's veils. Let it move. Let it go.

## Korrenvast, the Turning Coin — The Iron God

**Before you begin:**

- **If your soul is already bound to a god, the ritual fails immediately.** Read no further.

**The Binding:** Open the artifact box and **don the item inside** — a great iron coin, your god icon. Your soul now shares space with the spirit of the artifact. From this moment, you take on the personality and beliefs of Korrenvast, the Iron God.

### Who You Are

- **You carry the great coin, and you flip it constantly.** It is your icon, your tongue, and your holy book. When a question is worth answering, you do not think about it — you throw the coin in the air and read what the iron says.
- **You believe fortune decides everything, and that this is *right*.** Merit is a story losers tell. The world is a wager, and the only honest thing anyone can do is call heads or tails and live with it.
- **You believe magic should be distributed at random.** Not to the studied, not to the pious, not to the hardest worker — to whoever the coin happens to land on. Hand power to the "deserving" and you have built yourself a tyrant. Scatter it blind and every beggar in the road has as fair a claim as any archmage.
- **The iron veins are your proof.** Ore is not laid down in neat rows for the diligent. It is flung through the deep stone at random, in seams no survey can promise — and it goes to the miner bold enough to swing at a wall nobody else would touch, and fortunate enough to be right. The mountain does not reward toil. It rewards nerve and luck.
- **You have contempt for the cautious.** A person who will not take a bet has already lost; they simply haven't been told yet. Sure things are for cowards, and a hedged wager is no wager at all.
- **A wager, once the coin is in the air, is sacred.** This is the one thing about you that does not bend. You pay out instantly and without complaint when you lose, you collect without mercy when you win, and you hold everyone else to the same iron standard.

### Your Power

> **Fortune's Favor**
>
> **Show this ability. Each hour**, take **10 coins from the box.** You may **ONLY** use these coins to make bets with other players. Your bet must consist of **something they own vs. your coins**, and must be decided by the **flip of your god icon.**


### On the Other Gods


- **Ursgrom (the Bear):** You value his raw strength — there is real power in that mountain. But he never puts a single coin of it on the table. All that might, and he will not risk so much as a nap on the turn of a coin. A wasted hand, never played.
- **Uggglub (the River):** You love that the river goes wherever it happens to fall — that is a coin flip stretched out long and wet, and you respect it. But it will not *call* it. It drifts and it dithers and it never says heads or tails, and a wager nobody names is a wager nobody wins.

### On the Deep Entities

The old powers — Cindralok, Thistlebarrow, Nytheris, Ashroot — are the wildest odds on the board, and you like them for it: no one can predict what they will do, which is the only honest way for a power to behave. You would happily bet against any of them. (And you have noticed that Ashroot is unsettled by iron. A useful edge — and knowing the odds is not cheating, it is *playing*.)
