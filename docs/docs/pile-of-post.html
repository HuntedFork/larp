Print DOUBLE-SIDED. Each post is two pages: the first is the DO NOT OPEN UNTIL cover (addressed to The Librarian), the second is the letter inside. Flip on the SHORT edge. Then fold each sheet in half across the short edge (horizontal crease at the middle) with the cover facing out. You get an 8.5 × 5.5 folded letter: cover on the front, note hidden inside. Staple the open edge. There are 4 posts (open at 12:00, 1:00, 2:00, and 3:00 PM). Tuck the noted attachments inside before stapling: Clue 11 in post 1, the Ritual of Binding in post 2, and the Asking the Gods ritual in post 4.

Post 1 — Inside letter (prints on the back side)

Hello!

I'm a Junior Truth Seeker doing investigations down the river and it is an honor and pleasure to be able to write to you even though we have never met. During my researches, I have encountered some incredible effects.

The people here have their superstitions, certain gestures they make and words they say, ostensibly for luck — you know how it is with the locals. But lately I've begun to see some remarkable effects from these! As though they have begun to work. I've seen old wives' remedies (like muddling salt and the purple flower of the amaranth plant, which usually only provides a stinging sensation in its victim) heal deadly cuts with remarkable speed before my very eyes.

I hope to include additional notes soon, but I thought I would return this document I checked out to the library while I focused my research on these new effects.

Humbly yours,

Aldric Penn, Junior Truth Seeker

Attached: the returned document (Clue 11).

Dear Honored Librarian,

My research into the magic of the Old Ways has been most fruitful. I'm finding that not only are the effects of simple superstition amplified, but also ancient texts thought to be the ravings of madmen — or simply dismissed as superstition — are actually rituals. And not light ones at that. The houses of the locals often have a ritual book, which they claim contains family recipes and prayers.

But I've found these rituals are sometimes much more interesting than you would expect. Take for example the ritual attached to this message, which I unfortunately cannot attempt myself, as I do not have one of the associated objects.

Yours in study,

Aldric Penn, Junior Truth Seeker

Dear Honored Librarian,

I have seen a ghost today. I do not mean I was startled or frightened — I do legitimately mean that I witnessed a spirit. It wails outside my walls even as I pen this message. I do not know what it wants, but the family I stay with insists we not communicate with it, as it is likely to be hostile, and nothing I have seen indicates that they may be wrong.

Additionally, the animals of the area have become… strange. Large and frightening. Something is causing this place to become more dangerous. The family wonders what they will do if it gets any worse, and I feel for them. They have no weapons and cannot even read this book which I translate.

I write both to warn you and to ask for any help you can send. Anything which can defend these people from the greater threats they face will be appreciated — though I am sure it is the same way the region over.

In hope,

Aldric Penn, Junior Truth Seeker

Dear Honored Librarian,

I cannot write all that I wish to. I hope only that this missive arrives with you safely. The ritual contained within may be our only hope of salvation, for soon disaster may fall upon us. I would perform it myself, but alas I have no ghost peppers on hand. The world has become full of magic — too full.

I will pack my things and head to Chicken's Rest immediately, though I will be slower than this messenger, whom I command to rush with all haste.

Yours in haste,

Aldric Penn, Junior Truth Seeker
