# Summoning a Knight of the Lost Army

_Annotations by Scholar Ezra._

I was in the woods searching for the tomb of Aldric, when I stumbled upon a tombstone half-swallowed by roots and moss. It was not Aldric's — the name had been worn away entirely — but carved into its face was something far more interesting than a name.

The tombstone was cracked and weathered. Parts of the inscription were illegible, but the ritual itself was remarkably well preserved, as though the stone resisted decay around those words specifically. I have transcribed below everything I could read.

---

Here lie the riders of the Lost Army, who charged against the Fey and were cut down to the last. Their spirits never left these woods. They wander still, restless and furious, bound to the earth by the manner of their dying.

## Requirements

- The caster must have a **weapon** to offer the spirit. Place it on the ground before you.
- A seeker, to search for the spirit in the deep woods. (Out of game: The seeker will become the new ghost knight. They must leave the play area, and return having read the ghost knight instructions and wearing a white headband to indicate their new status.)

## The Summoning

### Part One — The Seeker's Words

Before departing into the woods, the seeker speaks:

> *Knights of the lost charge, riders who fell beneath the Fey's hand,*
> *I offer myself as seeker — a living soul to wander your woods in your stead.*
> *In exchange, I call one among you forth from the mist.*
> *Here is a weapon. Here is a purpose.*
> *Rise, and serve.*

The seeker then walks into the woods to begin their search. (Out of game: The seeker leaves the play area and returns as the ghost knight.)

### Part Two — The Caster's Welcome

When the ghost knight appears, the caster speaks:

> *Welcome, rider of the Lost Army.*
> *I am your commander. My voice is your will.*
> *Take up this weapon, and show me your moves.*


---

_I copied this inscription as faithfully as I could. When I returned to verify my transcription, the tombstone was gone — the ground undisturbed, as though it had never been there at all._
