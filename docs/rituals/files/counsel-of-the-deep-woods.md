# The Counsel of the Deep Woods

*If you are reading this, turn back. There are safer ways to die.*

Of all the rites set down by mortal hand, there is none so perilous as this, and none so powerful. Kings have burned libraries to keep it from being copied. The Collegium at Greymire struck its very name from their catalogues and pretended it never was. I set it down here only because a secret buried is a secret lost, and some wounds in the world are too deep to be closed by the counsel of any living soul.

For this rite does not summon a spirit, nor a demon, nor any thing that a warded circle might hold. It reaches down into the black heart of the Deep Woods of Chicken's Rest and calls upon **the elder powers themselves** — the old gods who wore this land as their skin before the first human drew breath, and who will wear it still when the last of us is dust and forgotten. They are not spirits to be bound. They are not demons to be bargained with as equals. They are the flood, the wildfire, the hollowing of the mind, the rot of the grave — forces older than language, wearing shapes too vast for the eye to hold. To call even one of them is to knock upon a door behind which something ancient stirs, turns, and *notices you.*

Understand what you are doing. You are not asking a wise elder for advice. You are a single ant, standing at the foot of a mountain, screaming a question up at the storm — and praying the lightning that answers strikes beside you and not through you. The powers may grant you truth beyond the reach of any mortal knowing. They may also strip the sanity from your skull, hollow your kin, drown your village, or answer with a truth so terrible it unmakes you. **They give nothing freely, they forgive nothing, and they never, ever forget.** Come prepared to the letter, or do not come at all — for a rite half-worked is not merely wasted. It is an insult flung into the face of a god, and the powers repay insults with a patience that outlasts generations.

Do not work this rite for idle curiosity. Do not work it to settle a wager, to win a lover, or to slake a fool's pride. Work it only when your need is so dire that the risk of annihilation is the lesser danger. Even then — think again.

This rite is generic; it may petition any one of the powers, or several at once. Know this, though: **each power you call multiplies the danger, for each is a separate abyss, and each must be honored perfectly or all is lost.**

## Drawing The Path

First, trace upon the ground the sacred symbol of the Deep Woods — the winding path, drawn exactly as it appears here, without a single break or stray line:

The path must be drawn large enough to walk, with room at its heart for the sacred draught. A flaw in the line is a flaw in the wall between you and them. Draw it as though your life depends upon its truth — because it does.

## The Circles of Summoning

Along the path, draw **one small circle for each entity you wish to contact** — no more, no fewer. Draw not a single circle beyond your true intent, for an empty circle is a mouth left open, and the powers are ever hungry for an invitation carelessly given. To call a power you did not mean to call is a mistake few live long enough to regret.

## The Offerings

In each small circle, place **an offering suitable to the entity** whose circle it is. What honors one power is a mortal insult to another; there is no safe or general gift. Know beyond doubt who you are calling and what that power hungers for. A fitting offering opens its ear and stays its wrath for the space of a question. An unfitting one — or none at all — turns its whole terrible regard upon you, and there is no offering after that will save you.

## The Calling

Standing upon the path, **call out to each entity in turn by its name, and name aloud the offering you have laid for it.** Speak the names clearly and without a tremor, though every instinct in your body will beg you to run. The powers do not lean in to listen to mumblers, and to falter over a god's name is to spit upon it. Once the first name leaves your lips, there is no stopping. Finish what you have begun.

## The Question

When each has been called and each offering named, **ask your question** — plainly, and but once. Ask for counsel, and counsel alone; the powers answer questions, they do not grant wishes, and a demand dressed as a question will be recognized and punished as the theft it is. Choose your words as though each one costs a year of your life. It may.

## The Sacred Draught

Finally, **place a potion brewed of Amaranth and Ghost Pepper in the center of the path.** This draught — the deathless flower married to the undying fire, the soul bound to the flame — is the price of an answer and the very door through which the answer comes. It is the one thing in all the rite that the powers cannot refuse and cannot ignore. Without it, every name you spoke and every offering you laid is nothing but a mortal shouting into a storm, and the Deep Woods will keep their silence — or worse, break it on their own terms.

If the path was true, the circles honest, the offerings fitting, and the draught pure, then an answer will come — in dream, in sign, in a voice heard by the marrow and not the ear. **Take what you are given and speak no further.** To thank them is presumption; to demand more is suicide. Lower your eyes, leave the wood, and do not look back to see what watches you go.
