# Ghost Invasion

**Characters:** 5 ghosts (scalable — run as few or as many as you have players for)

The veil has thinned over Chicken's Rest, and the restless dead have wandered into town. Each ghost carries an unfinished piece of their old life. They are not here to slaughter — they are here because they are *stuck*, and until the living help them, they cannot move on.

Each ghost is played independently. There is no single villain and no single ending. The living win, one ghost at a time, by figuring out how to lay each spirit to rest.

## Running the Mod

- **Every ghost must first read the [Ghost Player Instructions](../indymods/indymod.html?file=ghost-instructions).** That covers the universal rules: intangibility, immunity to weapons, speaking like a distant memory, and the "fade away" escape hatch if you stop having fun.
- **Each ghost then reads only their own section below.** Do not read the other ghosts' sections — part of the fun is the living discovering what makes each one tick.
- **Ghosts wear a white headband** to mark them as spirits.
- **Ghosts drift into town separately**, or emerge from the woods at different times, so the living deal with them one at a time rather than all at once. A little overlap is fine and chaotic and good.
- **Three of the ghosts hand out curse cards** (the Counter, the Madman, and the Judge). Print the [Ghost Curse Cards](../mods/ghost-curse-cards.html) sheet before the mod and give each of those ghosts their stack of 8 cards.
- **When a ghost is laid to rest**, they announce quietly that they fade into mist, remove the headband, and return to play as their own character. No questions asked.

## The Lover

*Jilted at the altar. Left standing in a chapel that emptied around them. They died still waiting, and they wander the world now looking for someone — anyone — to love them back.*

### Backstory (share slowly, in fragments)

You were to be married. You wore your finest. You stood at the altar and you waited, and you waited, and the guests filed out one by one, and still you waited. They never came. You do not remember dying. You only remember the waiting, and the terrible certainty that if you can just find the right person, this time they will stay.

### How You Behave

- You are **desperate for affection and terrified of being left again.** You latch onto whoever shows you the smallest kindness.
- You are **not menacing — you are pitiable.** You cling, you plead, you follow. You are the guest who will not leave.
- You speak longingly of love, of weddings, of being chosen.

### Your Curse — *Bound to the Bride*

- **One person at a time is bound to you.** Choose someone and declare them bound: *"You. You will not leave me. Not like they did."*
- The bound person is **forced to make physical contact with you and be dragged around wherever you wander.** (Out of game: keep it to a held hand, a linked arm, or a grip on the sleeve — nothing uncomfortable, and drop it instantly if the player taps out.)
- **Only one person is bound at a time.** If you bind someone new, the previous person is released.
- The binding **does not end when the person struggles or asks nicely.** It ends only when you are laid to rest — or when someone offers you what you have been waiting for.

### How to Lay You to Rest

- **Someone tells you they love you** — and means it enough to convince you. Once you believe you are truly loved, you can finally rest. Fade with relief and gratitude.
- **Optional — The Magic Ring:** If a magic wedding ring prop is available, someone slipping the ring onto your finger and saying vows is an instant, guaranteed release. *(See "Props" below.)*

## The Counter

*Counts things. Frantically. Madly. Endlessly. And cannot, no matter how hard they try, count past ten.*

### Backstory (only if they somehow get you to stop counting)

You do not have time for backstory. You are **counting.** There is always more to count and never enough time and if you lose your place you have to start over and you **cannot lose your place.**

### How You Behave

- You are **constantly, frantically counting something** — cobblestones, blades of grass, buttons, fingers, clouds, chickens. Out loud. "One. Two. Three. Four..."
- **You cannot count higher than ten.** Every time you reach ten, you falter, panic, and start over at one. This torments you.
- You become **extremely, disproportionately irate at anyone who interrupts you** — including anyone who tries to ask you about yourself, your name, or why you're counting. Interruption means starting over, and starting over is unbearable. Snap at them. Shoo them. Refuse to engage until they leave you to your count.

### Your Curse — *You've Forgotten Something*

When someone interrupts, pesters, or annoys you badly enough, **thrust a curse card at them** and go back to counting.

> **You've Forgotten Something:** You are suddenly, gnawingly certain you have forgotten something — or misplaced something important — but you cannot for the life of you remember what. This lasts **15 minutes, or until the Counter is gone**, whichever comes first.

Hand out cards freely to anyone who keeps bothering you. You have 8 of them.

### How to Lay You to Rest

- **Help you count something that is genuinely hard to count** — and count it *correctly, past ten,* with your help. A flock of milling chickens. A fistful of coins. The windows on a building. The stars. Leaves on a branch.
- Because you can't get past ten alone, someone has to actually count *with* you, keep you on track, and get you to the true total. When the count is finally, correctly completed, the compulsion breaks. Sigh, at peace, and fade.

## The Knight

*A duelist obsessed with honor. Came to Chicken's Rest chasing one more worthy fight — and found, at the end of a life of fighting, that the pursuit was hollow.*

### Backstory (share in weary fragments)

You lived for the duel. Honor, glory, the clash of steel — you thought it was everything. You came here seeking one last worthy opponent, one fight that would mean something. But every fight ends the same, and none of them ever filled the emptiness. You died as you lived: sword in hand, unsatisfied.

### How You Behave

- You are **proud, martial, and obsessed with honor and worthy combat.** You challenge people to duels. You speak of glory, of honor, of the beauty of a fair fight.
- Underneath the bluster you are **weary and hollow.** You half-suspect none of it ever mattered, but you don't know how else to be.
- You **look down on cowards and on unworthy, sloppy fighting.** A real challenge is the only thing that stirs you.

### Your Curse — *The Duelist's Blade*

- If someone refuses your challenge, mocks your honor, or simply won't give you a real fight, **you hit them with your sword.** (Out of game: slow, obvious, controlled swings only — you are a ghost, per the ghost rules. Pull your blows. Safety first.)
- You are not trying to murder the town — you are trying to *provoke a worthy fight.* Menace, don't massacre.

### How to Lay You to Rest

- **Give you a genuinely worthy fighting challenge** — something with real style and stakes, not a lazy swing.
  - A player who **fights you in a genuinely cool, committed, theatrical way** can satisfy you. Honor the drama; if someone brings real flair to the duel, let it move you.
  - Or the players can **summon a Ghost Knight to fight you** — see the [Summon Ghost Knight ritual](../rituals/ritual.html?file=summon-ghost-knight). A duel against a fellow spirit of a lost army is exactly the worthy fight you've been chasing.
- When you finally get the fight you were looking for — win or lose — you understand at last that it was never about winning. Lower your sword, at peace, and fade.

## The Madman

*Utterly, gibbering mad. Spouts insane nonsense at everyone. But buried in the babble are true names no living soul should know.*

### Backstory

There is no backstory. There is only the madness. You are a madman.

### What You Know (the important part)

Somewhere along the road to ruin, your shattered mind brushed against the **Deep Woods of Chicken's Rest**, and the names of its elder powers lodged in your skull like splinters. You babble them constantly, mixed in with nonsense so no one can tell truth from raving.

**Work these true names into your ravings** (mangle them, repeat them, scream them):

- **Cindralok** — the serpent coiled of soot and ember, who is *Flame and nothing but flame*.
- **Thistlebarrow** — the great stag, Lord of Horn and Branch.
- **Nytheris** — Keeper of the Glass Veil.
- **Ashroot** — the Rootbinder, whom *iron unsettles*.
- **Ursgrom** — the Slumbering Mountain, the bear-god of the long sleep.
- **Uggglub** — the Drowned Choir, the fish-god of the deep river.
- **Korrenvast** — the Turning Coin, the iron-god of the lucky seam.

You don't understand what these names mean. You just *know* them, and they claw their way out of your mouth. Sprinkle them among your insane ramblings so that a clever, patient listener can pick the real names out of the noise.

### How You Behave

- You **spout insane nonsense constantly.** Conspiracies. Prophecies. Warnings about the moon eating the bread. Whatever. Commit to it.
- You are **unpredictable and harmless-seeming**, but the things you say have teeth.

### Your Curse — *Spreading Madness*

When someone engages with you, listens too long, or challenges your ravings, **hand them a curse card.**

> **Spreading Madness:** You believe whatever insane thing the Madman just said — completely and sincerely — for the next **~30 seconds.**

Hand these out with glee. You have 8 of them.

### How to Lay You to Rest

- **Explain some real lore to you** — the true nature of the Deep Woods, the elder powers, the gods, anything genuine about the world. When a living soul speaks *true knowledge* to you, the noise in your head briefly quiets, and for one clear moment you understand. That moment of clarity is enough to let you go. Grow calm, lucid, grateful — and fade.

## The Judge

*A magistrate in life, and a hanging one. He passed sentence on hundreds and never once doubted himself. He is still holding court, and every living soul in Chicken's Rest is on his docket.*

### Backstory (deliver as rulings, not confessions)

You sat the bench for thirty years. You never lost a night's sleep over a verdict, because you were **right** — you were always right, that was the whole of your gift. It was only at the very end, with the black cap in your hand and nobody left in the gallery, that the thought arrived: *what if one of them wasn't guilty?* You died before you could finish the thought. You have been trying to finish it ever since, by convicting everyone else instead.

### How You Behave

- You **accuse people, loudly and formally, of sins.** Not crimes — **sins.** Pick one at random and pronounce it as settled fact. *"You. Yes, you. Pride. It is written all over you. How do you plead?"*
- **Sins to draw from:** Pride. Greed. Envy. Wrath. Gluttony. Sloth. Vanity. Deceit. Cowardice. Ingratitude. Blasphemy. Miserliness. Idleness. (**Not lust** — leave that one off the docket entirely.)
- **You do not investigate and you do not need evidence.** You are the Judge. The accusation *is* the verdict. If they protest, that is contempt, and contempt is itself a sin — note it and add it to the charges.
- You are **pompous, sonorous, and utterly certain.** You bang an imaginary gavel. You call the street to order. You address people as "the accused" and refer to yourself as "this court."
- **You never accuse anyone of the same sin twice in a row** — keep working down the list, so the town hears a spread of charges rather than one note.
- **If the Justice is in play, they are your favourite target.** A living magistrate holding court in *your* town is an outrage. Accuse them of pride first, and often.

### Your Curse — *The Verdict*

When you pass sentence on someone, **hand them a curse card.**

> **The Verdict:** You are crushed by guilt over something you have genuinely done. You cannot deny the charge, argue with the Judge, or stand up to him — you can only agree that he is right. Lasts **15 minutes, or until the Judge is gone.**

- **The guilt is real and it is theirs.** They pick the thing they feel guilty about — in-character or invented on the spot. You don't need to know what it is. Your job is to look grimly satisfied when they crumble.
- Hand these out freely to anyone who catches your eye, argues with you, or simply walks past the bench. You have 8 of them.
- **A cursed person can still act** — they can walk, talk, trade, and go about their day. They simply cannot look you in the eye and tell you that you are wrong.

### How to Lay You to Rest

- **Someone must stand up to you — *through* the curse.** Not a person you haven't sentenced yet, and not someone waiting out the 15 minutes: someone **currently under The Verdict** who refuses the charge to your face anyway. Being told off by the innocent means nothing to you. Being told off by the guilty, who know exactly what they've done and stand up regardless, is the thing that finishes the thought you died on.
- **Pushing through the curse requires an in-game ability** — a power, an item, a potion, a god's favor, anything on a character sheet that gives them standing. Raw willpower is not enough; the curse is stronger than that.
- **But trust the player.** If someone says an ability of theirs lets them defy you, **it does** — do not audit their character sheet, do not haggle, do not ask to see the card twice. If they've brought something and they're committing to the moment, give it to them.
- When it lands, do not argue. Go quiet. Sit down. Say something like *"...then this court was wrong. At least once. At last."* Fade with the enormous relief of a man finally allowed to be mistaken.

## Props

- **White headbands** — one per ghost. Available in the grab-bag binder.
- **Ghost Curse Cards** — print the [Ghost Curse Cards sheet](../mods/ghost-curse-cards.html). Contains 8 **"You've Forgotten Something"** cards (Counter), 8 **"Spreading Madness"** cards (Madman), and 8 **"The Verdict"** cards (Judge), 8 to a page. Give each of those ghosts their stack.
- **Magic Wedding Ring** *(optional, for the Lover)* — if a 3D-printed ring prop is available, it becomes a magic marriage ring the Lover can be laid to rest with. If you want, the Lover can even carry it and offer it to whoever will marry them, so the living can literally wed the ghost to release it.

## Special Considerations

- **Scale to your group.** Run one ghost or all five. Stagger their arrivals so the living aren't overwhelmed.
- **Ghosts are not out to win.** You are stuck, and you *want* to be helped. Give the living real openings to solve you. If they're close, lean in; don't stonewall a good-faith solution.
- **Reward cleverness and drama.** If a player finds a solution that isn't listed but clearly fits the spirit of your haunting, let it work.
- Every ghost should review the [Ghost Player Instructions](../indymods/indymod.html?file=ghost-instructions) before playing.
- After the mod, return all headbands and curse cards to the grab-bag binder.
