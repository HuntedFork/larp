# The Play

**Characters:** 1

- **The Noble** — a lord of considerable wealth and considerable boredom, come to Chicken's Rest in search of culture.
  - Dress as finely as the costume area allows. You are visibly richer than everyone here.
  - **Carries 20 coins.** This is the prize purse — see *Reward*.

## The Story

You are a patron of the arts. You have funded operas, endowed statues, and once paid a poet to follow you for a summer. You have come to Chicken's Rest because you hear things *happen* here, and you intend to commission a play worthy of the town.

You will pay an **exorbitant fee — up to 20 coins — to the people who put on the best play.**

Above all, you would like to see a story of something **historical**: a great deed that has actually happened, either **here today** or at **some other game**. A battle, a duel, a haunting, a scandal — real events, immortalized on the stage. Myths and invented tales will entertain you, but *history* is what opens your purse.

## Plot

1. **Arrive in town** and announce, loudly and to anyone who will listen, that you are commissioning a play. The purse is 20 coins. The best performance wins it.
2. **Name your terms.** You want a story of something historical — something that truly happened, here or at another game. Let the players know this preference plainly; a true tale delights you more than any invention.
3. **Let the town scramble.** Do not organize the plays yourself. Players must find actors, write parts, and wrangle an audience. Be seen watching the preparations with interest.
4. **Watch every performance.** Take a seat of honor. Applaud, weep, or yawn as befits each play. You may ask the players to settle the order of performances amongst themselves.
5. **Judge and pay.** When the plays are done, declare a winner with as much ceremony as you can muster, and pay out the purse.

## Judging

- **A true story earns your favor.** A reenactment of something that happened today — the beastmen's coming, the brawl over the antidote, a duel, a haunting — or of some famous deed from another game, is what you came for. Say so often enough that at least one troupe attempts it.
- **Quality still matters.** A stirring performance of a lesser tale can beat a lifeless performance of a great one. Reward acting, costumes, and nerve.
- **The purse is yours to divide.** All 20 coins to one troupe, or split among several — your call. More than one good play is good for the arts.
- **Praise is free; spend it lavishly.** Even the worst play gets a kind word. Especially the worst play.

## Reward

**The 20 coins are meant to be spent.** Every coin of the purse should leave your hands for a performance — do not leave town with it. If only one play is put on, it wins the whole purse. If many are put on, split it as you see fit, but give the best share to the play you loved most.

## Special Considerations

- **No combat.** You are a soft noble with no interest in fighting and no ability at it. If threatened, pay, flee, or loudly demand the Constable.
- **You are not a mark.** Every coin leaves your hands in exchange for art. If someone simply demands the purse, be scandalized.
- **Announce early, perform late.** Make the commission known as soon as you arrive, but hold the performances near the end of your visit — the more of the day's history that has happened, the better the material.
- **Recruit the Bard.** If someone sings for a living, make a point of telling them about the purse personally.
- **This pairs well with other mods.** If a performance is interrupted by an invasion, a brawl, or a falling star, that is simply theatre. Wait it out, then demand the show go on.

## Props

- **20 coins** — the prize purse.
