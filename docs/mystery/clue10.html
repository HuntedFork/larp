A primer from the Library of Chicken's Rest, shelved under The Alchemical Art: Of the Six Reagents, and the Elements to Which They Answer.

The first rule of the reagents

The apprentice alchemist learns this before any spell or sum: that the whole art rests upon six reagents, no more and no fewer, and that each of the six answers to a single element — one reagent to one element, never two reagents to the same, never one reagent serving two. This much is settled and beyond dispute. Which reagent answers to which element, however, the honest primer will not set down here — for that knowledge is dear-bought, guarded by apothecaries and cooks and crooked merchants, and a student who wants it must go and earn it. We give the six names, and no more.

The six reagents

The reagents of the art are these six, and only these:

Ghost Pepper, the red-hot southern pod;
Vile Glomphidious, the foul distilled fluid;
Rock Salt, the grey salt of the deep stone;
Dogwheat, the coarse roadside grain;
Amaranth, the flower that will not fade;
and Bone Dust, the honest white powder of ground bone.

The six elements

And the elements to which the reagents answer are the six known to every scholar — Fire, Water, Earth, Air, Spirit, and Body — one apportioned to each reagent, so that the six reagents and the six elements answer to one another exactly, with none left over and none doubled.

(a reader's note in pencil:) Six reagents, six elements, one each — same shape as the powers. Coincidence? Or is someone matching them up?

Here ends the primer. For the particular pairings, seek the recipes, rulings, and journals of those who work the art.
