A grease-spotted page torn from a cook's book, the corners chewed. In a broad, confident hand: Gramma Pell's Own Receipt for Wailing Stew — for when the guests have outstayed their welcome.

Wailing Stew (serves six, or curses one)

Folk turn up their noses at my cooking until they've had a bowl of this, and then they don't turn up anything ever again. It is not a kind supper. But a recipe is a recipe, and mine are the best in three valleys, so mind the steps and don't you dare substitute.

Begin with a whole Ghost Pepper — and it must be the Ghost, for it is a thing of pure Fire and nothing else carries the flame into the pot. Char it black over the coals till the whole kitchen weeps, then drop it in. A milder pepper gives you a milder curse, which is to say no curse at all, only heartburn. If it is Fire you want cooked into the dish, it is the Ghost you reach for.

(scrawled sideways:) Gloves, you fool. I lost a fingernail to this stew and I'd not lose you one too. — G.P.

Now for the broth. Pour in a good ladle of Vile Glomphidious — that watery, foul-smelling fluid is the very essence of the element of Water, and it is what makes the stew run cold and deep no matter how you boil it. Hold your nose; it stinks like a drowned bog, which is only proper, for the Water is its whole nature. Nothing else will thin the stew the right way. Sea-water, rain-water, honest well-water — none of them carry the element true. It must be the vile fluid.

(a different, shakier hand below:) Made this for cousin Bertran. He is much quieter now. Recommend.

Simmer till it screams. Serve to no one you love.
