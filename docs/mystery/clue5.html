A letter, water-stained at the fold, sent to a cousin in the lowlands by one Merrit Cawl, a pilgrim who wintered at Chicken's Rest.

Cousin —

You will not believe where the road has left me, and you will believe still less what the folk here worship. I went down to the river at the ferry-crossing to see the shrine they all speak of, and I am not ashamed to tell you I knelt in the mud like the rest of them.

They call him Uggglub, the Fish God, and cousin — the Fish God is the lord of the Water and of nothing else; the whole of the river, the still pool, the rain, and the flood are his and answer to no other power. The ferryman would not push off without a coin dropped in the current for him, and when I asked why, he only said that every drop from the mountain snow to the sea passes through the Fish God's hands, and a wise traveller keeps on his good side.

(smudged, in the same hand:) Do not laugh. The one year they forgot his festival, the ferry-crossing flooded to the eaves. They have not forgotten since.

I have seen his image now, carved above the shrine: a great blunt fish with too many eyes, and every one of them turned toward the water. Whatever else these hill-folk have wrong, they have the river's master named right. Write soon, and keep dry — a thing easier said here than done.

Your cousin, Merrit
