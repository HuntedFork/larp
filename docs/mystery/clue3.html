Filed in the record-book of the Magistrate of Chicken's Rest: The Matter of Sedwin Halloway, Merchant, against a Complaint of Fraud brought by Corvis Thorn, Bone-Setter.

The Ruling

Both parties being heard, I set down the matter plainly.

The complainant, the bone-setter Thorn, states: that he bought of the merchant two goods for his workings and was cheated in both. In his own words, taken down: "He sold me sea salt and swore it was Rock Salt. Any child of the craft knows the element of Earth answers only to true Rock Salt, the salt of the deep stone — sea salt is a thing of Water and will not serve an earth-working at all. And worse, he sold me common chalk ground fine and called it Bone Dust! The element of the Body answers only to true Bone Dust, honest ground bone — chalk is dead stone and will knit no flesh, mend no frame, do nothing for a Body-working. I am a bone-setter; I know my bone from my chalk."

The defendant, the merchant Halloway, states: that "salt is salt and white powder is white powder, and if the man cannot tell the difference that is his lookout, not mine." He does not deny the substitution.

(margin, the clerk's hand:) Note: the defendant priced the chalk as though it were bone. Greed, not error.

Judgment

The merchant Halloway is found guilty of false advertising, having sold sea salt as Rock Salt and chalk as Bone Dust, both proven false by their very elements. He shall pay a fine of twelve silver to the town and restore the complainant's coin.

Yet the complainant is not blameless. By his own testimony Thorn sought to purchase true Bone Dust — that is, ground human bone, the trade in which is forbidden by the town's law. Corvis Thorn is therefore to be arrested for the attempted purchase of illegal bones. A man who knows his bone that well knows too well where it comes from.

So ruled, and entered into the record this day. — The Magistrate
