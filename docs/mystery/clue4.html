A leaf folded into the crypt-box of the saint, in a hand none of the town can name. It is not to be read until the bones are made whole.

The Rattling of the Bones

As the last dusty bone slots into place, the skeleton begins to rattle. The bones shiver and knit, joint finding joint with a sound like dice in a cup, and the dust of long years falls away from them like ash off a stirred fire. Then the skeleton sits up — slow, patient, in no hurry at all — and it turns its skull toward you, and it begins to speak, in a voice like wind through a chapel keyhole.

Hear me, and do not be afraid. I am Merewen, whom your grandmothers' grandmothers named Saint, though I was only ever a woman who could not bear to leave a spirit troubled.

I was born in this valley, and my gift showed early: the troubled came to me — the grief-sick, the guilt-ridden, the ones whose own minds had turned against them — and I sat with them until the thing that gnawed them was named, and the naming laid it. For know this: the restless are of two kinds, and I tended both. There are the spirits that walk free — the unhoused dead, drifting and cold — and there are the spirits that live on inside people, the ghosts a living body can carry. I laid both kinds to rest. My right hand I set upon the troubled, and under it they unburdened; my right foot carried me down every dark road where something walked that should have been sleeping.

Only once did my gift fail me. When the great hollowing came upon the beast-kin, my hand could not mend it — that hunger was too old and too vast for one woman, and it was the Mask, not I, that drove it from the land. I tell you this so you do not mistake me for more than I was.

(in the same hand, smaller:) They keep my right hand and my right foot as relics still, wrapped in cloth. Rest them upon the troubled; the gift has not wholly left them.

When my years were spent they buried me here, in the town I loved, and I have slept content. One thing only I ask of you who have woken me, and then I will gladly sleep again: dress my crypt with Amaranth — Amaranth, the Flower of the Spirit, for I so love it's color. Let the flower that outlasts the flesh keep watch over mine.

The skull's jaw closes. The bones lie back down, and are still.
