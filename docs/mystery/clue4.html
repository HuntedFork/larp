From the private journal of Halbrecht Vunn, apothecary of Chicken's Rest — a loose leaf headed On the vexing question of the Amaranth, the flower that will not fade.

The vexing flower

I have all my other reagents settled and set down elsewhere — the pepper, the vile fluid, the salt of stone, the ground bone, each married to its proper element and proven a hundred times. But the Amaranth has plagued me for a year. I knew it belonged to some element, for a reagent so potent must; I simply could not say which. So I did what I always do: I ruled out what it is not, and trusted the last door standing to be the true one.

I need not test it against Fire, Water, Earth, or Body — those elements are already wed to their own reagents, and no element takes two. That leaves only two doors the Amaranth might yet open: the open Air, or the Spirit. And so I put it to the test.

The Amaranth has no part in any working of the open Air. I have wound it into every wind-charm and breath-spell I know, and it lies dead in the hand every time — the Air will have nothing to do with it. That door is closed, then, and firmly.

(margin:) And no, before some fool asks — it will not knit bone either; I proved it useless to the Body long ago. The Body is bone's country, not the flower's.

So the reckoning is done, and simpler than I feared. Fire, Water, Earth, Body — all spoken for. The Air will not have it. Only one door remains unshut, and so the Amaranth must be the reagent of the Spirit — the dream-flower for the working of the soul. Of course it is. The bloom that will not fade, given to the part of us that outlasts the flesh. I should have seen it a year ago and saved myself the petals.

Settled at last. Now let me sleep. — H. Vunn
