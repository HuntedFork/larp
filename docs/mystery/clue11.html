Copied from the consecration rolls of the chapel at Chicken's Rest, in the clerk's fair hand, and kept among the chapel records.

The Record of the Consecration of Saint Merewen

Let the record show that in the year three hundred and seventy-one, the people of Chicken's Rest, with one voice and no dissenting soul among them, consecrated Merewen of this parish a Saint, and set her name among the blessed dead.

Her renown is written plainly, for there was not a soul in the valley who had not seen it: the Saint could put spirits at rest — and she rested both kinds: the spirits that dwell within people, the griefs and guilts and madnesses that hollow the living, and the spirits that walk free, the unhoused dead who drift where they are neither wanted nor warm. The troubled came to her door and left it quiet; the walking dead came to her and walked no more. In all her years she turned no restless thing away.

Of her relics the record says this: her right hand, that quieted the troubled at a touch, and her right foot, that carried her to every restless place, were kept by the town and wrapped in cloth, and it is held that the gift has not wholly gone out of them.

And when she died, full of years, she was buried here among us, in Chicken's Rest, beneath the red tablecloth, and the cloth is kept over her place always, as she asked.

(clerk's note:) The red cloth is changed when it fades and is never lifted, save by those who would speak with her. Let whoever lifts it lift it gently.

So recorded and sealed. — the Clerk of the Chapel
